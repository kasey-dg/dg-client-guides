Getting Around London

Getting around London is easy once you know the trick. The Underground (the Tube) is the fastest way to move around the city — and you don't need a special card. Simply tap your contactless credit card or phone (Apple Pay or Google Pay) directly on the yellow readers at the gates. It charges you automatically at the best rate for your journeys. If you'd prefer a black cab, they're the real London experience — iconic, safe, and hailable anywhere on the street. Just stick your arm out. Uber also operates throughout the city if you prefer an app.

West End Theater

The Lion King

26 years in the West End and it still stops the room. The opening sequence — Pride Rock rising, the sun flooding the theater, animals streaming down the aisles — is one of the most breathtaking moments in all of live theater. Best for first-timers, families, and anyone who needs proof that live performance can move you in ways a screen simply cannot. At the Lyceum Theater.

🗓 Book Ahead

Hamilton

Lin-Manuel Miranda's genre-defining musical about America's founding fathers, told through hip-hop, R&B, and rap. Still one of the hottest tickets in London after nearly a decade. Funny, heartbreaking, jaw-dropping, and genuinely unlike anything else you'll ever see on a stage. At the Victoria Palace Theater.

The gold standard. Running in London since 1985 — the longest-running musical in West End history — and it hasn't lost a single ounce of power. Top Parks

Hyde Park

London's great backyard — 350 acres right in the center of the city. Rent a pedalo on the Serpentine, swim at the Lido in summer, catch an open-air concert, or simply collapse on the grass with a coffee. Home to Hyde Park Winter Wonderland in December.

★ Free entry

St. James's Park

The oldest and most beautiful Royal Park, surrounded by Buckingham Palace, St. James's Palace, and Horse Guards. Stroll the lake path for the most iconic view of Buckingham Palace anywhere. At 2:30pm daily, resident pelicans are fed fresh fish. Yes, pelicans. In central London.

★ Free entry

Regent's Park

Elegantly landscaped and home to the magnificent Queen Mary's Rose Garden — 12,000 roses at peak bloom in June. Also home to London Zoo, an open-air theater running Shakespeare all summer, and Primrose Hill just to the north for the city's best skyline views.

★ Free entry

Hampstead Heath

Wild, vast, and utterly unlike anywhere else in London — 790 acres of ancient woodland, meadows, and ponds. Climb to Parliament Hill for a breathtaking unobstructed skyline view. The brave swim in the famous bathing ponds year-round. Nearby Kenwood House has a stunning free art collection.

★ Free entry

Greenwich Park

The oldest enclosed Royal Park, on a hill above the Thames with spectacular panoramic skyline views. Home to the Royal Observatory — stand on the Prime Meridian with one foot in each hemisphere. Pairs perfectly with a Thames River Cruise from Westminster.

★ Free entry

Richmond Park

The largest Royal Park at 2,500 acres — more wilderness than park. Home to 600+ free-roaming red and fallow deer among ancient oaks. Visit the Isabella Plantation woodland garden and enjoy views all the way to St. Paul's Cathedral on a clear day.

★ Free entry
Good to knowSt. James's Park pairs perfectly with Buckingham Palace; Greenwich Park is a natural combo with a Thames River Cruise. For the best of Hyde Park, go on a weekday morning. And if you visit Richmond Park during rutting season (October), the deer are extraordinary — but keep your distance!

A fully preserved 18th-century townhouse where you wander silently through rooms frozen mid-moment. The candles flicker. The tea is still warm. Part museum, part performance art. One of London's most singular, spellbinding experiences.

Highgate Cemetery

Karl Marx, George Michael, and alleged vampire legends — all in one dramatically overgrown Victorian cemetery. The atmospheric West side is guided-tour only and straight out of a gothic novel. Genuinely breathtaking.

🗓 Book Ahead

Jack the Ripper Tour, Whitechapel

Creep through the gas-lit cobblestone streets of East London where Victorian London's most notorious unsolved murders unfolded in 1888. Expert Ripperologists lead you to the actual crime scenes, sharing facts, conspiracies, and the social conditions of the era. Some tours use RIPPER-VISION™ projectors to bring the atmosphere alive; the best ones end at the legendary Ten Bells pub — a site directly linked to the victims — where you can discuss the final theories over a pint. Not for the faint of heart. Absolutely unmissable for true crime obsessives.

Day Trips from London

Stonehenge & Bath

The classic combination — and genuinely unbeatable for first-time visitors to England. Stonehenge is 5,000 years old and still completely mysterious; standing inside the stone circle at dawn on a private tour is one of those experiences that changes how you think about human history. Bath pairs perfectly — a stunning Georgian city built around ancient Roman baths, with incredible architecture and great restaurants. Easily done as a day trip from London.

🗓 Book Ahead

Windsor Castle

The world's largest and oldest inhabited castle, just 40 minutes from London by train — and the weekend home of the Royal Family. The State Rooms are extraordinary, the art collection is world-class, and St. George's Chapel is where King Charles III was married and where Queen Elizabeth II is buried. A half-day is plenty; pair it with an afternoon in the charming town of Windsor itself.

🗓 Book Ahead

The Cotswolds

The England of your imagination — honey-colored stone villages, rolling hills, thatched rooftops, and sheep wandering across country lanes. Bourton-on-the-Water, Burford, and Chipping Campden are the standout villages. This is the day trip clients request most often after they get home and wish they'd done it. Private tours are worth every penny here — a good guide will take you well off the beaten path.

🗓 Book Ahead

Warner Bros. Studio Tour — Harry Potter

The actual sets, costumes, and props from all eight Harry Potter films, housed in the studios where the movies were made. The Great Hall, Diagon Alley, Platform 9¾, Dumbledore's office, the Forbidden Forest — all real, all extraordinary. A must for any Harry Potter fan of any age, and genuinely magical even if you're not. About 20 miles from central London; shuttle coaches run from Watford Junction.

Iconic Pubs

Americans know that Brits love their pubs — but most visitors don't realize that a proper British pub is something entirely different from a bar back home. It's a living room, a history lesson, a community hub, and sometimes a Michelin-starred meal, all under one roof. Some of the pubs below are 400 years old. Some have been visited by Dickens, Churchill, and Samuel Johnson. Some are serving the best food in the city. All of them are worth your time.

Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese, Fleet Street

Built in 1667 on the ashes of the Great Fire of London — though there has been a pub on this site since the 1530s. The cellar is 13th-century monastery. Samuel Johnson wrote his famous dictionary around the corner and drank here daily. Mark Twain, Arthur Conan Doyle, and Charles Dickens were all regulars. Duck through the narrow entrance off Fleet Street and descend into a warren of low-beamed rooms and snugs that feel completely untouched by the last three centuries. Genuinely one of the most atmospheric rooms in London.

★ Historic legend

The George Inn, Borough

London's last surviving galleried coaching inn, dating to 1676, now owned by the National Trust. The timber-framed balconies overlooking the cobblestoned courtyard are extraordinary — in Shakespeare's day, traveling theater companies performed in yards exactly like this one. Dickens mentioned it in Little Dorrit and was a known regular. Grab a pint in the courtyard on a sunny afternoon and you'll feel the centuries peel back. Two minutes from Borough Market.

★ Historic legend

The Prospect of Whitby, Wapping

London's oldest riverside tavern, with records going back to 1520 — when Henry VIII was on the throne. It was once the drinking den of pirates, smugglers, and cutthroats (replica hanging gallows still stand on the foreshore as a nod to those days). Today it's a charming Thames-side pub with original flagstone floors, a pewter-topped bar, and views across the river from the first-floor balcony that are genuinely stunning. Well worth the short trip east.

★ Historic legend

The Devonshire, Soho

Crowned the UK's number one gastropub for 2026 by the Estrella Damm Top 50 Gastropubs — the highest accolade in British pub dining. In the heart of Soho, it's the rare pub that's as good for a serious meal as it is for a casual pint. The food is inventive, seasonal, and genuinely exceptional. Book a table rather than just turning up — this one is a destination, not a pitstop.

★ #1 UK gastropub 2026🗓 Book Ahead

The Harwood Arms, Fulham

The only Michelin-starred pub in London — full stop. Tucked away in Fulham's backstreets, it looks like a perfectly ordinary neighborhood boozer from the outside. Inside, the food is quietly extraordinary: seasonal British ingredients, game-focused menus, and cooking that would hold its own in any fine dining room in the city. Three courses for around £79 is remarkable value for a Michelin star. One of London's great insider secrets.

★ Michelin starred🗓 Book Ahead

The Lamb & Flag, Covent Garden

The oldest pub in Covent Garden, tucked down a narrow alley off Rose Street — the kind of place you only find if you know it's there. It has been licensed since 1623, was once called the Bucket of Blood (bare-knuckle fights upstairs), and the poet John Dryden was famously beaten up outside. Today it's a warm, characterful local that fills up with theatergoers and in-the-know tourists. The perfect pre- or post-show drink in one of London's most charming pub interiors.

★ Hidden local gem

Popular Neighborhoods

Americans visiting London for the first time often recognize the names — Soho, Notting Hill, Mayfair, Chelsea — but not many know how wildly different they actually are from one another. London isn't one city; it's a collection of villages, each with its own personality, pace, and vibe. Here's your cheat sheet.

Covent Garden

The postcard-perfect heart of tourist London — and honestly, worth every bit of the hype. The Victorian market building buzzes with street performers, boutique shops, and some of the city's best people-watching. Home to the Royal Opera House, the London Transport Museum, and enough restaurant options to keep you busy for a week. Neal's Yard — a tucked-away courtyard splashed in technicolor — is one of London's most charming hidden corners.

★ First-timer favorite

Soho

London's most gloriously chaotic square mile. By day it's film production companies, indie coffee shops, and some of the city's most exciting restaurants. By night it's Theaterland, legendary cocktail bars, and a nightlife scene that has no interest in winding down early. Carnaby Street, Chinatown, and Berwick Street Market are all on your doorstep. If you love food and energy in equal measure, this is your neighborhood.

★ Food & nightlife hub

Mayfair

London at its most unapologetically glamorous. Bond Street is the shopping address — Cartier, Burberry, and every luxury house you can name. The streets are lined with Michelin-starred restaurants, five-star hotels (The Connaught, Claridge's, The Dorchester), and private members' clubs. Grosvenor Square, Mount Street, and the intimate streets around Berkeley Square are perfect for a leisurely stroll between indulgences.

★ Luxury central

Notting Hill

Those pastel-colored houses are absolutely real, and they're every bit as pretty as the film promised. Portobello Road Market is one of the world's great antique markets — best on Saturday mornings when the whole street comes alive with vintage treasures, street food, and flowers. The neighborhood feels like a village that somehow got dropped into a capital city — tree-lined streets, neighborhood pubs, and a community feel that's rare in central London.

★ Most photogenic

Marylebone

London's best-kept secret neighborhood. While tourists stream past on Oxford Street, Marylebone High Street quietly gets on with being one of the most charming shopping streets in the city — independent bookshops, beautiful delis, and the kind of cafes you want to sit in for hours. Home to the Sherlock Holmes Museum, Regent's Park is on the doorstep, and the whole area feels like a proper village in the middle of the city.

★ Local favorite

South Bank

The most walkable stretch of river in London. From Westminster Bridge all the way to Tower Bridge, the South Bank delivers a non-stop procession of culture — the Tate Modern, the Globe Theater, the BFI, the Southbank Center, and Borough Market all strung together along the Thames. The riverside promenade is magic at any time of day, and sunset views across to St. Paul's Cathedral from here are genuinely breathtaking.

★ Riverside culture

Shoreditch

Where London goes to be cool. Banksy got his start here. Alexander McQueen had his first atelier here. Today it's the city's creative epicenter — world-class street art around every corner, Brick Lane's legendary curry houses and vintage shops, Spitalfields Market on weekends, and a restaurant scene that rivals anywhere in the city (Brat and Rochelle Canteen, both in our restaurant section, are right here). Best explored on foot with no real plan.

★ Creative & edgy

Piccadilly & St. James's

Grand, historic, and proudly old-school London. Piccadilly Circus is the neon-lit crossroads of the city — not the most beautiful corner of London, but you absolutely have to see it. A short walk away, St. James's Street is the address for the city's oldest institutions: Fortnum & Mason (founded 1707), The Ritz, Berry Bros. & Rudd wine merchants, and gentlemen's clubs that have barely changed since the 19th century. This is the London of tradition.

★ Classic London

Chelsea & King's Road

Chelsea has been London's most fashionable postcode since the Swinging Sixties, and it's never really stopped. King's Road is the main artery — a mix of upscale boutiques, excellent restaurants, and the kind of effortless style that feels native here. The Chelsea Physic Garden is one of London's most beautiful hidden secrets (oldest botanic garden in London, founded 1673). Sloane Square and the streets around it feel like a neighborhood that knows exactly how good it has it.

★ Effortlessly chic

Kensington

Museum Row is here — the Natural History Museum, the V&A, and the Science Museum are all within a five-minute walk of each other, which is a frankly unreasonable amount of brilliance in one postcode. Kensington Palace sits at the edge of the park (Princess Diana's former home), and the streets around Kensington Church Street are ideal for antique hunting and independent restaurants. South Kensington in particular has a distinctly Parisian feel — wide boulevards, beautiful buildings, great patisseries.

★ Museums & elegance

Greenwich

Technically south-east London, but easily worth the journey — and the best way to arrive is by Thames River Cruise from Westminster, which is an experience in itself. Greenwich is where time literally begins: the Prime Meridian runs through the Royal Observatory here, and you can stand with one foot in each hemisphere. The market, the Cutty Sark clipper ship, the gorgeous park, and the sweeping city skyline view from the top of the hill make this one of London's most rewarding half-day trips.

★ Half-day trip
Good to knowThe beauty of London is that you can walk between most of these neighborhoods — Covent Garden to Soho is five minutes on foot, Mayfair to Hyde Park Corner is ten. Build your days around neighborhoods rather than individual attractions, and the city starts to make a lot more sense.
